Subject: ODbL notice/public-use treatment of non-reconstructive on-chain geographic commitments
Dear Licensing Working Group,
We operate Boulder Roller, a commercial cycling product. We compile a geographic database of defined climbs from OSM road geometry and licensed elevation sources. We treat our published climb registry as an ODbL Derivative Database and are implementing complete, free, machine-readable, versioned exports with attribution and source notices. Independent rider observations and economic records are stored separately.
We would welcome a focused licensor interpretation, not a review of our business model or a request to avoid share-alike.
For each published climb, a public Solana registration contains a compact commitment. Its current 64-byte encoding contains a format byte, a company-assigned UUID, a canon version, reserved bytes, and a BLAKE3 digest of the canonical profile bytes. It does not contain coordinates, a compressed geometry encoding, or OSM way IDs. A proposed future format would also commit by hash to the centerline, gates and corridor.
The digest lets a reader check separately obtained geographic bytes; it does not supply or reconstruct those bytes. The identifiers resolve through our public service to the versioned geographic export. Our implementation policy is to make that complete export and its notices available before registration. Our own explorer will visibly attribute OSM and link the exact release; general third-party blockchain explorers may display only the raw event/account fields. The current commitment field itself has no space reserved for human-readable attribution or a URI.
Under ODbL §§1, 4.3, 4.4(c) and 4.6, does publication of this non-reconstructive commitment itself constitute Publicly Using the Derivative Database, or publication of a Produced Work from it, independently of the accompanying geometry API and map? Would including more geographic fields within the hashed input change that interpretation?
If the commitment itself has a notice obligation, would a durable public resolver from its immutable identifier/version/commitment to an attributed, version-specific export manifest, together with visible attribution in our explorer, satisfy the reasonable-association requirement? Or does the Foundation expect attribution text or a licence/source URI to be included in the registration event/account itself, given that third-party explorers may not follow an off-chain resolver?
We are not relying on hashes to keep any publicly used geographic derivative closed, and are not proposing to publish commitments to our private candidate atlas. The question is whether the immutable wire format needs additional notice fields despite the full public data offer. We can provide one specimen event, its exact byte description and the corresponding release manifest once the export is live.
A pointer to applicable published guidance, or permission to retain and refer to your written interpretation, would be helpful. We understand that a response is not an amendment of ODbL or approval of unrelated elevation-source licences.
Regards,
Tim, Boulder Roller
